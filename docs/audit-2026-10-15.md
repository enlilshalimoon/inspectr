- **Live site:** read-only look at uselookover.com (landing, pricing, signup). An unauthenticated call to the AI route redirects to /login (correct).

## Verdict (plain language)

**Lookover handles the first five minutes well. It isn't yet good enough for an inspector to hand a real client report to a buyer.**

The basic loop works in code: sign up, type an address, add a photo, get a sensible draft finding in under a minute, approve it, and send the client a link and a PDF. Today's fixes covered the biggest reasons people used to stall (the onboarding wall, iPhone photos, retries, empty screens). Someone from an ad who tries it with three photos from their camera roll should have a good experience.

The trouble starts once an inspector uses it on a real job, which is what decides whether they pay $79 a month:

1. **Every photo is filed under "Grounds & Site" unless they change a dropdown before each shot.** The AI knows the photo shows an electrical panel, but the report puts it under Grounds. A first real report will look wrong.
2. **When reviewing a draft, they can't see the photo it came from.** Photos sit in a separate strip, so they're approving text about a photo they have to hunt for.
3. **The client report shows every photo, including the ones the inspector deleted findings for.** None of the photos are attached to the finding they illustrate.
4. **Photos can be lost on a bad signal** (attics, basements, rural Texas). The website promises offline capture, but it isn't built.
5. **They can't write a finding without a photo,** like "no smoke detector in bedroom 2", and can't add Pool, Fireplace or Outbuildings sections.
6. **Texas inspectors are generally required to deliver on the state's TREC form (REI 7-6).** Lookover's report is its own format. Confirm this with Kristy or another Texas inspector this week, because it could decide whether any Texas inspector can use Lookover for paid work. [suspected; regulatory, not code]

There's also one security issue to fix before more people sign up. **Any logged-in user can mark their own account as paid** with one line in the browser console, because the database lets users edit their own billing fields. It's an easy fix.

**Recommendation:** keep the iPhone ad at its current budget, but don't scale until fixes 1–5 below are in. They're small and medium jobs, roughly a week of focused work. In parallel, settle the TREC question, since it decides whether Texas is the right market for the ads. The 5-seat team inquiry (Barkleys Roofing) is a good sign, but a roofing team will hit problems 1, 2 and 5 on their first day.

## 2. Quality of the AI output

**Pipeline** (`lib/ai/anthropic.ts`, `lib/ai/prompts.ts`, `app/api/ai/process-photo/route.ts`): Haiku 4.5 reads the image and returns JSON (subject, section, defects, equipment data). Sonnet 4.6 then writes one finding from that JSON plus the voice transcript and property context. Whisper (`whisper-1`) does transcription. **[verified]**

What's good:
- The prompts are well written. They trust the inspector's voice over the vision model, ban dollar amounts, ban the word "dangerous", forbid naming contractors, use "indicates possible…" instead of diagnosing causes, and keep severity conservative with a short explicit list of safety hazards. **[verified]**
- There's a 29-case benchmark grounded in the InterNACHI SOP, with a recorded 86% baseline (`db/benchmark`, DECISIONS.md). **[verified it exists; not re-run, no keys]**
- The image type is detected from the actual bytes, with a plain-English HEIC message. Failures carry a `message` written for the inspector. **[verified]**

Concerns, in order:
1. **The drafting model never sees the photo.** Sonnet only gets Haiku's text summary, so the finding's detail is capped by what the cheaper model noticed (`anthropic.ts:141-147`). Sending the image to the drafting call, or using one Sonnet call with the image, would likely raise quality more than any prompt change. Re-run the benchmark with images first; the current benchmark is text-only. **[verified architecture; quality impact suspected]**
2. **One photo always means one finding.** A photo showing two defects gets one finding. Three photos of the same leak get three findings. A context photo still gets an "info" finding the inspector has to clear. **[verified]** Let vision return "no reportable finding" (skip the draft), and let one draft cover several photos.
3. **The no-cost-estimates rule is enforced only by the prompt.** No code checks the output for `$`, "cost", "estimate" or price ranges before saving (`process-photo/route.ts:197-212`). The benchmark says the prompt holds, but a cheap check that flags or strips such text, plus a warning badge in Review, would turn a STATUS rule into a guarantee. **[verified: no check exists]**
4. **AI output isn't validated.** Both calls run `JSON.parse(...) as Type` with no schema check (`anthropic.ts:135, 154`). An out-of-range severity fails the database check and comes back as an "AI took too long" message. A malformed `ai_analysis` gets cached on the photo and reused forever (`process-photo/route.ts:64-65, 106`). Use structured output (tool use or a JSON schema) plus zod. **[verified]**
5. **Timeouts:** no `maxDuration` on the AI routes and no timeout on the Anthropic or OpenAI clients (the SDK defaults are 10 minutes with 2 retries). In practice Haiku plus Sonnet takes about 10–20 seconds, so it's fine. Under rate limiting, retries can hit Vercel's function limit and the inspector sees "took too long". The client handles that error gracefully. **[suspected]**
6. **There's no per-user rate limit** on `/api/ai/*`, and those routes skip the entitlement gate (it only covers `/inspections*` pages, `lib/supabase/middleware.ts:6`). An expired-trial user with a session could keep calling the AI. Low risk. **[verified]**

**What a bad draft looks like to the user:** a confident, well-written finding filed under the wrong section (fix #1), shown without its photo (fix #2), sometimes describing a context shot as an "Informational" finding. The writing itself will rarely be the problem. The problem is that it doesn't look like the inspector's report.

## 3. Correctness and security

**Auth in server actions and routes:** every server action and API route checks the session (`getUser`) or relies on row-level security (RLS) through the session client. Mutations with no explicit user check (`updateFinding`, `setFindingApproved`, `approveAllInSection`, `deleteFinding`) are still protected by RLS through `owns_inspection`. Admin actions check `isAdmin`. Cron requires `Bearer CRON_SECRET`, dry runs included. The Stripe webhook verifies its signature on the raw body. **[verified]**

**RLS:** enabled on every table. Inspections are scoped by `inspector_id = auth.uid()`; child tables use the `owns_inspection()` helper. One inspector can't read or change another's rows through the API. **[verified in code]**
- **Billing columns are user-writable.** Fix #6. **[verified in code]**
- `users_update_own` also lets a user set `company_logo_url` to any string, and `updateLogo` (`app/(app)/settings/actions.ts:71-88`) accepts any path. The public report then signs that path with the service-role key (`app/(client)/report/[slug]/page.tsx:100-106`). An inspector could point their "logo" at another inspector's PDF in `report-assets` and get a signed link to it through their own report. They'd need the other user's and inspection's UUIDs, so this is low risk, but restrict the logo path to `${uid}/logo.*`. **[verified in code]**

**Storage:** both buckets are private. Policies scope objects to `{uid}/…` (`0002_rls_policies.sql`, storage section). Photos and PDFs are served only through signed URLs (1 hour on reports, 5 minutes for AI fetches). **[verified]**

**Public report link:** 8-character random slug (31^8 ≈ 850 billion possibilities); only finalized or delivered inspections render; only approved findings are shown. It does leak:
- **every photo in the inspection** (fix #3) **[verified]**
- the client's name and the inspector's phone and license (intended)
- edits made after delivery (fix #9)

The page shows no client email or notes. **[verified]**

**Report email** (`review/actions.ts:100-218`): any signed-in user, trial included, can finalize an inspection with zero findings (the server allows it), then send unlimited emails to any address with up to 2,000 characters of their own text, sent from `reports@uselookover.com` under any "company" name they choose. With email confirmation off, that's a spam and phishing path through your verified domain, which could hurt deliverability for everyone. Fix: require at least one approved finding, rate-limit (for example 10 sends per day per inspector), and optionally require a confirmed email before the first client send. **[verified in code]**

**Other email paths:** welcome and password reset go through `sendSystemEmail` (sent after the response, best-effort). The reset uses `generateLink` and our own `/auth/confirm` link. The origin comes from the request `Origin` header, but Next 16 rejects server actions whose Origin doesn't match Host (`node_modules/next/dist/docs` → Server Actions security), so this isn't exploitable. Using `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL` would still be cleaner. Founder emails reply to a personal Gmail address hard-coded at `lib/email/send.ts:29`. Now that hello@ is a real mailbox, consider moving to it. **[verified]**

**Cron:** fail-closed when `CRON_SECRET` is unset; the nudge logic is unit-tested; deduplication uses `app_metadata`. **[verified]**

**Stripe:** the webhook is correct for the main events, and `reconcileBilling` heals missed webhooks on the billing page. `checkout.session.completed` sets `active` even if payment is still pending (for example a delayed payment method). That's minor for cards. **[verified]**

**Admin:** gated by the `ADMIN_EMAILS` env var. Email confirmation is off, so if an address on that list ever lacks an account, anyone could sign up as it and get /admin. Keep every admin address registered. **[verified in code; low]**

**Headers:** HSTS is present. There's no CSP or `X-Frame-Options` (the report page could be framed). Low risk. **[verified live]**

## 4. Reliability

- **Uploads go directly from the phone to Supabase Storage,** not through Vercel, so there's no function timeout or body limit on photos. **[verified]** Photos are resized to at most 2048px before upload, which helps on slow signal. **[verified]**
- **Data-loss risks:**
  - photos in a failed or pending state live only in page memory (fix #5)
  - the regenerate path deletes before replacing (fix #7)
  - a failed transcription tells the inspector to re-record (fix #7)
  - there's no `beforeunload` guard anywhere in the app **[verified]**
- **Bad mobile connection:** each upload is a single attempt. A failure shows "Upload failed. Check your signal" with Retry. There's no automatic retry or backoff and no on-device queue. **[verified]** The AI call is a separate request after upload. If it fails, "Try again" is shown, and the next Capture visit re-tries automatically (the same mechanism behind fix #4). **[verified]**
- **Fan-out:** opening Capture fires one `/api/ai/process-photo` call per undrafted photo at the same time (up to 100). Picking 20 photos fires 20 drafts at once. Anthropic rate limits will cause retries and slow, partial failures on larger jobs. Use a small concurrency limit (3–4). **[verified in code; impact suspected]**
- **Function timeouts:** no route or server action sets `maxDuration`. The AI routes are probably fine. **Finalize (PDF render with every photo embedded) is the most likely to time out** on a 40–80 photo inspection. **[suspected]** Consider `export const maxDuration = 60` on the finalize page, and smaller embedded images (for example 1024px) for the PDF.
- **Error states:** Capture, onboarding and new-inspection errors are now readable. Review voice errors show raw messages (`finding-card.tsx:224`), and the Review recorder still forces `audio/webm` when it can't detect a format (`finding-card.tsx:482-493`). The Capture version was fixed to let the browser choose; this one wasn't. **[verified]**
