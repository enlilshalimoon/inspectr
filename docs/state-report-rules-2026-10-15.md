# Home inspection report rules by state, for Lookover

Researched October 15, 2026. Primary sources (statutes, admin codes, board sites) were used where they could be reached. Anything not confirmed from a primary source is marked **unconfirmed**. This is market research, not legal advice. Check each target state's current rule text before launching there.

## Summary

Texas is the only state found with a promulgated report form that must be reproduced verbatim (TREC REI 7-6). The one other form rule is South Carolina's (S.C. Code §40-59-560(A)): reports must be "on a form approved by the commission," but a form "containing the same information" also complies, and the commission doesn't appear to publish its form. Every other state either has no report rules (no-license states) or mandates **contents, not layout**. So a custom branded PDF like Lookover's is legal almost everywhere.

The real compliance gap is structural, not visual. Most licensed states (FL, OH, OR, VA, MA, WI, NJ, NY, IL, RI, CA, PA, and others) require the report to:

- list every system and component the SOP covers, with its condition and description;
- say which systems were **not inspected and why**;
- in several states, add fixed statements, the license number, signature and dates.

Oregon explicitly says a deficiency-only report does not comply.

Lookover's findings-first format therefore needs a **systems inventory section** (inspected / not inspected / reason, plus descriptions), a **compliance block** (license number, signature, SOP reference, required statements), and a few **state-specific add-ons** (NC summary page, VA smoke alarm and CSST text, PA conspicuous disclaimers, WI summary page and "defect" wording). Each is a toggle on one template, not a separate form.

No state rule specifically governing AI or software-drafted reports was found. Every state that regulates places responsibility on the licensed inspector who performs the inspection and signs the report, which Lookover's "inspector approves every finding" workflow fits. The search was not exhaustive (**unconfirmed**). No state found requires repair cost estimates. Florida (§468.8323) and Oregon (OAR 812-008-0204) expressly don't require them, and Wisconsin (SPS 131.33(2)) doesn't require repair costs. So Lookover's "no cost estimates" policy is safe.

## Top 8 states to target next (market size × readiness)

1. **Florida.** Probably the largest licensed inspector base in the US. Requirements are simple content rules (§468.8323) and cost estimates are expressly not required. Lookover fits after adding the systems-not-inspected section.
2. **California.** Huge market with no license and no report form. The only rule is the B&P §7195 report definition, so it's the lowest compliance friction of any big state.
3. **North Carolina.** Large, fast-growing market. The one hard requirement is the statutory summary page with fixed text plus license number and signature. Build it once as a toggle and NC is fully ready.
4. **Georgia.** Big, growing, unlicensed. No report rules, so it's ready today.
5. **Arizona.** Strong housing market, and the BTR checklist gives an exact component list to map into Lookover's systems inventory. That makes compliance easy to demonstrate in ad copy.
6. **Ohio.** Licensed since 2019 with clear, short report rules: license number and expiration, date, address, deficiencies with repair-or-monitor recommendation, not-inspected list. Pairs well with Midwest targeting.
7. **Pennsylvania.** Large market with no license. It needs the §7508 conspicuous disclaimers as a fixed block, which is low effort for a template.
8. **Virginia.** Solid, affluent market. Needs a VA pack (time and weather fields, smoke-alarm statement, CSST statement), all fixed text Lookover can insert automatically.

Alternates: Tennessee (simple rules, growing), Illinois (signature and license block only), Colorado (unlicensed, ready now). Defer: Texas (REI 7-6 template), South Carolina (form ambiguity), and states marked Unclear.

## What Lookover's PDF must add to be compliant in "Ready now" states

**Universal (build into the default template):**

1. **Systems inventory section.** For every system the state SOP covers (structure, exterior, roof, plumbing, electrical, heating, cooling, interior, insulation and ventilation, fireplaces, plus appliances where required), show:
   - the description or materials;
   - status: Inspected / Not inspected / Not present;
   - for anything not inspected, **the reason** (FL, OH, VA, MA, NJ, OR, WI, RI, TN, LA, AR).
   
   Oregon rejects deficiency-only reports, and most SOPs expect descriptions too.
2. **Recommendation on every finding.** Each finding needs "repair / monitor / further evaluation by a qualified professional", with a reason if not self-evident (FL 61-30.801, OH 4764.01(D)). Lookover's severity tiers map to this; add an explicit recommendation line.
3. **Inspection methods.** How the roof, crawl space and attic were observed (VA, MD, OR, MA).
4. **Header and identification block:**
   - inspector name;
   - **license or registration number** and **expiration date** (OH, VA, IL, AK, KY);
   - company and contact;
   - client name;
   - property address;
   - inspection date (VA adds start/finish times and weather);
   - report preparation and revision dates (WI).
5. **Signature block.** Inspector signature with license number directly after it (KY, IL, NC), plus supervisor and trainee co-signature support (DE, NC, IL).
6. **SOP reference.** Name the standard followed (state SOP, ASHI or InterNACHI) and a link or appendix to it (LA requires providing it; KY must match the SOP chosen at licensure).
7. **Standard limitations statement.** Visual, non-invasive inspection of readily accessible areas at the date and time of inspection; not technically exhaustive; no warranty or guarantee; not an appraisal; not a code-compliance inspection; environmental hazards excluded unless contracted (KY requires the environmental-hazards statement; PA requires the disclaimers conspicuously).
8. **Scope / limited-inspection notice** when not all systems are inspected (OH, MD 14-pt bold).
9. **Pre-inspection agreement support.** Signed before the inspection in LA, NY, OH, OR, TN, WA, VA, and others; NY has mandated clauses. Not part of the report, but Lookover should store or link the signed agreement or integrate with tools that do.
10. **Delivery timestamp.** Record when the report link or PDF was delivered. NC's deadline is 3 business days and LA's is 5 days.
11. **Confidentiality.** Client-only access by default (MA, NY, RI). The web link should be private or tokenized and shared only with the client's consent.

**State add-on packs (toggle by property state):**

- **NC:** summary page with the statutory statement, all items not functioning as intended or needing further investigation, license number and signature.
- **VA:** smoke-alarm statement, yellow CSST statement for pre-2008 homes, start/finish time and weather, "NRS" designation.
- **PA:** conspicuous §7508 disclaimer block, plus conspicuous flagging of unreasonable-risk defects (map to the "safety hazard" tier).
- **WI:** summary page; use the word "defect" exactly per Wis. Stat. 440.97(2m); material adverse facts; s. 440.975 disclosures.
- **KY:** environmental-hazards exclusion statement and an itemized exclusions list.
- **MD:** 14-pt bold limited-scope disclosure.
- **MA:** full 266 CMR 6.04 component identification list.
- **TX:** separate REI 7-6 template (out of scope for now).

**Policy notes:**

- Keep "no cost estimates". No state requires them, and several say they're not required.
- No state AI or software rule was found (**unconfirmed**, not exhaustive). Keep an audit trail showing the licensed inspector reviewed and approved each finding, and avoid marketing that implies the AI "inspects."
